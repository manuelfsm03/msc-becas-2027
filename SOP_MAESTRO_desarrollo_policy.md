## 1. La espina

Una sola cadena, y es la que hay que proteger de acá en adelante:

> **epistemología → cuándo algo es creíble → inferencia causal como la respuesta moderna de las ciencias sociales a esa pregunta → política pública que funciona porque está bien identificada → Ministerio de Economía / Banco Central.**

### 1.1 El hallazgo del 7-sep: los tres bloques son la misma escena

No son tres anécdotas de un CV. Es la misma operación repetida tres veces con las apuestas subiendo: **agarrar una creencia sobre la que alguien ya está actuando, someterla a evidencia, y devolverla corregida en una forma que esa persona pueda usar para decidir mejor.**

| Escena | Creencia sometida a prueba | Apuesta |
|---|---|---|
| TED-Ed, 17 años | el cambio climático es mentira (evidencia fabricada por él) | una sala de colegio |
| Bunge | el precio relativo maíz-soja drivea qué vende el productor | plata, decisiones de mesa |
| Docencia FCE-UBA | — | alumnos aprendiendo a distinguir evidencia buena de mala |

Esto resuelve el riesgo "la epistemología flota" de §2: ya no flota, tiene tres aterrizajes. Y contesta sola la pregunta del endgame, porque ese rol —el que chequea si la creencia sobre la que se está operando se sostiene, y la hace utilizable para el que decide— es la descripción de puesto de un área de evaluación de política pública.

### 1.2 Las dos piezas de materia prima

**(a) El hook — la charla TED-Ed del último año de secundaria.** Formato mockumentary: veinte minutos argumentando que el cambio climático era mentira, con evidencia fabricada, y a mitad de camino el desarme en vivo de su propio argumento. El tema nunca fue el clima: fue lo barato que sale hacer que algo parezca verdad.

⚠️ **Fue TED-Ed (programa de clubes escolares), NO TEDx ni charla TED de escenario principal.** Se dice "a TED-Ed talk I gave at my school". Dejar que se lea como TEDx sería inflación de credenciales en una carta cuyo tema literal es la fabricación de credibilidad. No se toca.

**(b) La bisagra — Epistemología de la Economía (Diego Weisman).** Acá está el upgrade estructural del 7-sep: el motor de la carta ya no es la anécdota de los 17 años, es un origen **adulto y académico**. Vía filosofía de la ciencia entendió por qué importa la economía y qué tiene que hacer una afirmación económica para merecer creencia; ahí leyó por primera vez a Duflo y a Angrist & Pischke. Se siguió alimentando en Desarrollo Económico (Juan Pablo González).

**Por qué esto funciona.** El SOP de desarrollo promedio dice "quiero reducir la pobreza en mi país". Es sincero y es indistinguible de otros cuatrocientos. Este dice: *me importa el problema del conocimiento, y la identificación causal es la epistemología aplicada de la política pública.* Es un ángulo que casi nadie ocupa, y es verificable en el CV.

## 4. Borrador — bloques 1, 2, 3, 4 y 6

> Construido **solo** con material confirmado por Manuel. Sin datos inventados. Los huecos van marcados `[NEED]`, no rellenados.

**Block 1 — Hook + thesis**

> At seventeen I spent twenty minutes convincing my secondary school that climate change was a fabrication. It was a TED-Ed talk. The charts looked real, the sources did not exist, and halfway through I took the whole thing apart in front of the audience, showing them line by line how each piece of evidence had been manufactured and how quickly they had accepted it.
>
> The subject was never the climate. It was how little it costs to make something look true.
>
> That question has organised my academic life since. At what point does a claim become credible enough to act on?

**Block 2 — La pregunta hecha economía**

> I did not have a discipline for it until a course on the epistemology of economics with Diego Weisman. Philosophy of science is what made economics matter to me. It was there that I saw what an economic claim has to do to earn belief, and there that I first read Duflo, and Angrist and Pischke. A course in development economics with Juan Pablo González kept it going.
>
> The most serious answer I found was in applied econometrics, in what Angrist and Pischke called the credibility revolution: the turn towards research designs that make a causal claim answerable instead of assumed. To me it reads as epistemology carried out with data. Identification strategies are how the discipline made itself accountable to the problem of knowing, and development economics is where that has been pushed hardest, because the cost of being wrong is counted in people. Policy built on correlation is policy built on manufactured evidence. It is the same failure I staged at seventeen, with the stakes moved.

**Block 3 — Evidencia I: investigación (IIEP / paper de biomasa)**

> At the IIEP I joined MESi, the applied modelling group at the University of Buenos Aires, and co-authored a study on biomass energy in the Argentine Mesopotamia, the provinces that hold most of the country's forest-industrial residue and almost none of its renewable-energy policy attention. It was my first time working with economists of that standing, and it raised the bar I hold my own work to. We built a regional input–output model and used it to weigh biomass expansion against the thermoelectric generation it would displace. Biomass came out ahead on every dimension we could measure: output, employment, emissions.
>
> What stayed with me was not the result. It was the paragraph where we wrote down what the model could not tell anyone. It could show how a shock travelled through a structure we had specified in advance. It could not show that any intervention had caused anything, because that was settled in the calibration before the model ever ran. A policy reader would not have known the difference unless we said so.
>
> So we said so. A model like that one can only hand back the structure you gave it: its coefficients are the assumptions, and the result is their arithmetic. It can be internally wrong, but it cannot be surprised. I want to be trained in the methods that can be, where the variation comes from the world rather than from me, and where a finding is allowed to overturn the belief that prompted it.

**Block 4 — Evidencia II: aplicada (Bunge)**

> At Bunge my work sits between the data and the people who trade on it, which mostly means testing what the desk already believes. The most useful thing I have produced there was a negative result. Traders held that the corn–soybean relative price drove the farmer's decision of which crop to sell. It is a good story. It has a mechanism, and everyone on the floor could recite it. I tested it `[NEED: método]`. It was not a driver.
>
> Telling a trading floor that its working rule is not in the data is a harder conversation than any I have had in a classroom. It is also the one I most want to be equipped for. A market-share series I started calculating on my own now runs as a daily report to the CEO for the Southern Cone, and commercial teams across the region set their decisions by it. `[NEED: alcance real, y si podés, una decisión concreta que cambió]`

**Block 6 — Endgame**

> I want to work on macroeconomic policy in Argentina, at the Ministry of Economy or the Central Bank. The objection to that comes from serious people. Replying to Angrist and Pischke in 2010, Christopher Sims held that natural experiments are rhetorical devices rather than experiments, and that what the credibility revolution claims about macroeconomics is mainly nonsense.
>
> I take it seriously. I also think the cliometric tradition has been quietly answering it for decades. The Federal Reserve Act split Mississippi between two districts that then ran opposite doctrines through the Depression, and the arbitrary border it left behind gave Richardson and Troost as clean a test of central bank intervention as macroeconomics is likely to get. Banks on the activist side survived at higher rates, and recovery came sooner. `[NEED: un ejemplo argentino equivalente — una frontera, un corte administrativo, un cambio de régimen provincial. Si no aparece uno bueno, se corta la oración y no se inventa.]`
>
> The part I already know I love is the last step. Teaching at the FCE-UBA is where I learned that an argument is only finished when someone who has to decide something can use it. At seventeen I showed a room how cheaply I could make them believe something. I would like to spend a career on the opposite problem.

## 9. Precisión terminológica: qué tipo de "experimental" (decidido 7-sep)

No son lo mismo y la carta tiene que elegir: economía **experimental de laboratorio** (Vernon Smith) · **field experiments / RCTs** (Duflo, Banerjee, Kremer) · **cuasi-experimentos y experimentos naturales** (Angrist, Card, Imbens).

El endgame es política macro en Ministerio/BCRA, donde el laboratorio no escala, y Mississippi es cuasi-experimental. **Escribir "quasi-experimental and natural-experiment methods"**, no "experimental economics" a secas: un lector UK lee laboratorio y ve incoherencia con el endgame. Los RCTs quedan mencionados como la escuela de la que salió el estándar, vía Duflo en el bloque 2.
