## HSG St. Gallen — MEcon · Public Policy

Ángulo: método causal y su escala macro, bajo un mismo techo. Profes: Lechner ✓ · Koeniger ✓ (10 sep). 184 palabras.

St. Gallen is the one place I have found where both halves of this question sit in the same building. At SEW-HSG, Michael Lechner's group develops causal machine learning for the evaluation of public programmes, and releases the tools, such as the *mcf* package, that let the method travel beyond the paper. Winfried Koeniger's work on how monetary and fiscal policy transmit, identified with high-frequency transaction data, takes that discipline to the level of the macroeconomy. That is exactly the step my own work stopped short of.

The specialisation in Public Policy would let me build the MEcon around that pairing: econometrics first, then policy analysis, with the GSERM course in Causal Inference as the bridge. I am also drawn to a department that treats advising government as part of its work rather than an afterthought, because that is the setting I am training for.

I would come to St. Gallen with a specific question: which of the policy claims I hear repeated in Buenos Aires would survive a design that could prove them wrong. I would like to leave able to answer it.

## Warwick — MSc Economics

Ángulo: cliometría: la identificación en el pasado. Profes: Ricco ✓ · Fenske ✓ · Gupta ✓ (25 sep). 173 palabras.

Warwick answers the question with history. The Global Economic History programme at CAGE, where James Fenske and Bishnupriya Gupta work, does for the past what I want to learn to do for the present: it finds the accidents of borders, institutions and colonial rule that let a causal claim be tested rather than asserted. Their recent work on colonial persistence is the same genre as the Mississippi study that convinced me macroeconomic questions can be identified at all.

The other half is Giovanni Ricco's work on monetary policy shocks. His argument, that the instruments macroeconomists usually rely on mix the true shock with the central bank's information about the economy, is the Sims objection taken seriously and answered with better identification. It is the closest thing I have found to the problem I want to work on at the Central Bank.

CAGE's Responsive Public Policy programme closes the loop from evidence to decision. Warwick is where I would learn both to find the variation and to be honest about what it can carry.

## Bristol — MSc Economics

Ángulo: treatment effects y policy learning. Profes: Han ✓ (25 sep) · Zylberberg ⚠️. 168 palabras.

Bristol has turned my question into an institution. The Centre for Evidence-based Public Services exists to make public decisions answerable to evidence, and the School's econometrics group lists causal inference, treatment effects and policy learning as a declared area. No other department I considered describes its purpose in those terms.

Sukjin Han's research is the most direct version of what I want to learn. His work on identifying treatment effects for policy evaluation, and more recently on policy learning that accounts for who gains and who loses, addresses exactly what the model in my biomass study could not: whether an intervention changed anything, and for whom. Yanos Zylberberg's work on spatial and development macroeconomics shows how those designs scale up to questions about regions and growth, which is where the Argentine Mesopotamia sits.

I want the MSc to give me the formal training to read, and eventually produce, work of that kind. Bristol is the programme where the econometrics and the policy purpose are taught as one subject.

## Southampton — MSc Economics

Ángulo: series de tiempo: la identificación propia de la macro. Profes: Pitarakis ✓ · Magdalinos ✓ · Olmo ✓ (25 sep). 170 palabras.

Sims's objection was never that macroeconomics cannot identify anything. It was that the toolkit of applied microeconomics does not transfer. Macroeconomics built its own tradition of identification in time series, starting with the vector autoregressions Sims himself introduced, and Southampton is one of the places where that tradition is strongest.

The econometrics group describes its work as spanning time-series methods, financial econometrics and machine-learning methods applied to problems in causal inference. Anastasios Magdalinos's work on inference in cointegrated and mildly explosive systems, and Jean-Yves Pitarakis's work on predictive regressions, are the kind of training a macroeconomic question actually demands: knowing when a relationship in the data is a finding and when it is an artefact of persistence. José Olmo's work in financial econometrics and monetary economics connects those methods to the central bank I hope to work for.

The other programmes I am applying to would teach me the micro answer to the credibility problem. Southampton would teach me the macro one, which is the one Argentina's policy questions need.

## Glasgow — MSc Economics

Ángulo: historia económica cuantitativa. Profes: Trew ✓ · Montoya-Blandón ✓ (25 sep). 151 palabras.

Glasgow's attraction for me is a department where quantitative history and careful econometrics are practised side by side. Alex Trew's work on infrastructure and the spatial take-off of the Industrial Revolution, and his recent finding that historical air pollution still shapes how neighbourhoods sort today, is identification by long memory: the past as a source of variation the present cannot manufacture.

Santiago Montoya-Blandón's teaching and research in applied and Bayesian econometrics would give me the other half, the formal discipline to state what a given design can and cannot support. His background in Latin America also makes him a natural reader of the questions I would bring from Argentina.

The Applied Economics cluster would be my home in the programme. I want to leave Glasgow able to ask of any regional policy in Argentina what Trew asks of the Industrial Revolution: what would have happened otherwise, and how we can know.

## Birmingham — MSc Economics NEED · confirmar programa

Ángulo: desarrollo, ambiente y datos regionales. Profes: Strobl ✓ (25 sep) · City-REDI ⚠️. 146 palabras.

Birmingham is the programme closest to the work I have already done. Eric Strobl's research on development and the environment uses natural events, such as hurricanes, as the source of variation, and measures their economic effects with satellite and climate data. It is the causal version of the question my biomass study could only answer structurally: what actually happens to a regional economy when its environment changes. ⚠️ [NEED · si el NDVI/MODIS fue proxy, una frase que lo conecte]

City-REDI, the University's institute for regional economic development, adds the policy end. It works with local and regional governments on the evidence behind their decisions, which is the role I want to play for the provinces of the Argentine Mesopotamia and, later, at national level.

I would use the MSc to acquire the econometric training that turns regional data into causal evidence, and I would like to learn it from people who apply it where the stakes are concrete.
